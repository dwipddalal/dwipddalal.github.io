Tweet 2: Forgetting

Standard BC trains one loss: imitate the demo actions. Nothing asks the backbone to keep knowing what "pink" means, so step by step it stops knowing. Try it on your model: swap two objects, repeat the command. Score: 2.3%. The VLA remembers the demos. It forgets the world.

Tweet 3: Anchoring

Forgetting is not a vision-encoder problem; it runs the full depth of the backbone. So pin every layer: keep a frozen copy of the VLM and distill its hidden states into the trainable backbone, layer by layer, while you finetune. No extra data, no new labels, no forgetting.

Tweet 4: Alignment

Co-training's losses never share a frame, so words and motion quietly disagree (0% on the swap). Fix: every action has a free direction word. Predict it on the very frame the action head acts on, through the frozen vocab head. Word-action agreement: 11.3%→65.8%.

Tweet 5: Significance

Read the results as a pattern, not a scoreboard: the more a test punishes memorization, the bigger the gain. Position-swap, where baselines score 0. CALVIN chains, where the gap grows 6x from step 1 to step 5. Same story on a 0.5B regression head and a 3B flow-matching DiT.

Tweet 6: Conclusions (text only)

Is it just regularization? Shuffle the direction labels or swap in nonsense words: every gain collapses to baseline. The lesson for VLA finetuning: action learning and the VLM's semantics are not at odds; tie them on the same observation. Anchor + Align, no extra data.
