Tweet 2: Forgetting

Standard BC trains one loss: imitate the demo actions. Nothing asks the backbone to keep knowing what "pink" means, so step by step it stops knowing. Try it on your model: swap two objects, repeat the command. Score: 2.3%. The VLA remembers the demos. It forgets the world.

Tweet 3: Anchoring

Forgetting is not a vision-encoder problem; it runs the full depth of the backbone. So pin every layer: keep a frozen copy of the VLM and distill its hidden states into the trainable backbone, layer by layer, while you finetune. No extra data, no new labels, no forgetting.

Tweet 4: Alignment

Every continuous action hides a free label: its direction word (up, down, left, right, forward, back). Predict the word through the frozen vocab head on the exact frame the action head acts on; the actions stay continuous. Word-action agreement: 11.3%→65.8%.

Tweet 5: Significance

The restored semantics generalize: train with the green mug, ask for the pink one at test, and Anchor-Align still does the task. Real xArm7: 28.3%→54.2% and 36.7%→60% on two different action heads. A gain that survives an action-head swap lives in the backbone, not the head.

Tweet 6: Conclusions (text only)

The takeaway for VLA finetuning: learning actions and keeping the VLM's semantics are not at odds. Anchor to preserve the semantics; align to tie words to motion on the same observation. The same-frame tie is the piece co-training cannot give. Both together, no extra data.
