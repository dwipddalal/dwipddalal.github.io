Tweet 2: Forgetting

Observation: catastrophic forgetting. A VLA finetuned with standard BC memorizes the demos and erodes the VLM's semantics: objects, colors, spatial relations. Change anything at test time and it breaks.

Tweet 3: Anchoring

Fix 1: Vision-Language Anchoring. Keep a frozen copy of the pretrained VLM and pin every backbone layer to it while BC learns the policy. The representations that generalize stay intact.

Tweet 4: Alignment

Fix 2: Language-Action Alignment. On the very frame the action head acts on, the model must also say the action's direction word through the frozen vocab head. What it says now matches what it does.

Tweet 5: Significance

Does it generalize? Position swap: 2.3% to 22.6%. LIBERO-Plus: 85.1% to 90.3%. Real robot: 28.3% to 54.2%. Word-action agreement: 11.3% to 65.8%. One recipe, everywhere we tested:

Tweet 6: Conclusions (text only)

Takeaway: generalizable VLAs need more than imitating demonstrations. Preserve the pretrained semantics (anchor), and ground them in the actions the robot executes (align). Paper, code, and rollouts: anchoralignvla.github.io
