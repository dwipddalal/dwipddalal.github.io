Tweet 2: reason 1, forgetting

Why does standard BC hurt generalization? 🤔 Reason 1: finetuning forgets simple semantic concepts. The same VLM backbone that moves to the green mug can no longer move to the pink mug when asked. Good old catastrophic forgetting strikes again!

Tweet 3: reason 2, disjoint data

Reason 2: scattered supervision. The VLM backbone gets language labels only in pretraining, on web-style data. The action expert gets action labels only in VLA finetuning, on expert demos of the target robot. The two heads never learn from the same observation. Anchor-Align converts the continuous action targets in expert demos into discrete action directions expressed in language, and co-supervises the VLM backbone and the action expert on the same robot observations.

Tweet 4: OOD-only simulation

We all love generalization, yet we keep testing on LIBERO (99% already, why, why, why 🫣). In this work, our Table 1 of sim results is exclusively OOD evaluations. 1. Tweet 5: robot evals

And lots of evals on a UFactory xArm7: spatial rearrangement, cluttered scenes, compositional object layout, and a semantic test (train on the green mug, ask for the pink one). 📈 7 held-out tasks, 20 rollouts each, 140 trials per model, two VLA architectures (VLA-Adapter, 0.5B regression head; StarVLA, 3B flow-matching head). Massive push by the undergraduate author @chahit, and a smooth checkpoint pipeline from @dwip.

Tweet 6: forgetting, measured

"Catastrophic forgetting" is the boogeyman many works throw punches at, but few back the claim. Let's do better. Two measurements: visual reasoning (GQA) during finetuning, and per-layer language preservation (text-token CKA to the pretrained VLM). Standard BC loses 94% of its GQA accuracy, and its CKA collapses in the output layers (0.34 at layer 24). With anchoring, every layer stays near the pretrained VLM.
